    Stable dynamic partial reconfiguration for edge AI

    VivadoVitis HLSAXI DecouplerPCAPBare-metal CPython

    A drone that runs one big CNN on every frame wastes energy on empty sky. Dynamic partial reconfiguration lets the FPGA swap models at runtime: a light Sentry CNN (2.1 ms per frame) while little is happening, and an INT8 YOLOv3-Tiny (8.4 ms per frame, 42.3% mAP@0.5 on VisDrone) when the scene gets busy.

    On real hardware, the first ten swaps all failed. Reconfiguring while the processor was still talking to the partition deadlocked the AXI bus, and the inrush current of freshly configured logic pulled VCCINT into brownout. Quarantining the partition behind an AXI Decoupler and throttling its clock from 50 to 10 MHz during the swap fixed both: inrush dropped from 2.45 A to 1.12 A and every swap completed inside one frame.

    A predictive, contour-based scheduler decides when a swap is worth it, cutting false triggers from 47.1% to 4.2%.

    Brought up NVIDIA's open-source NVDLA (nv_small) in the programmable logic of a Zynq UltraScale+, then built the full stack above it. The ARM cores program the engines over the CSB register interface on the AXI control path, while NVDLA streams tensors to DRAM over the DBBIF AXI data backbone. Linux sees it through a device-tree overlay.

    The kernel driver was written for Linux 4.19 and the board runs 6.6. Porting it across seven major kernel versions meant reworking memory management, interrupt handling and device binding until it loaded, bound and reset the hardware. On the model side, a pooling-free CNN trained on 60K MNIST images reached 98.26% and compiled to a 458 KB INT8 loadable, delivered with the Vivado design and a Jupyter demo as a reproducible package.

    U4SRAM_6T

    6T SRAM bitcell, full custom

    When

    Process
    180 nm CMOS, 1.8 V
    Flow

    284mVSNM at TT, 27 °C
    142.5pswrite access, 168.2 post-PEX
    4.15µWdynamic power

    VirtuosoADEAssura DRC/LVSQuantus PEX

    A memory cell has to survive being read and still give in when written. The pull-down NMOS is sized stronger than the access transistor, so the cell ratio is above 1 and a read cannot flip the stored bit. The pull-up PMOS is weaker than the access transistor, so the pull-up ratio is below 1 and a write can overpower it.

    The layout keeps the two half-cells mirror-symmetric to avoid mismatch and minimises poly and metal runs to cut parasitic capacitance. It passes DRC with zero errors, LVS reports matching netlists, and Quantus extraction showed the write slowing from 142.5 to 168.2 ps, still well within budget, before the layout was streamed out to GDSII.
